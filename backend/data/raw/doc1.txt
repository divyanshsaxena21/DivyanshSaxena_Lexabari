The Constitution of India guarantees fundamental rights to all its citizens.  
These rights are essential for the development of the individual and the protection of their dignity and freedom.  
Fundamental rights are justiciable, meaning that individuals can approach the courts if these rights are violated.  
The Constitution provides six main categories of fundamental rights:  

1. Right to Equality: Ensures equality before the law and prohibits discrimination based on religion, caste, gender, or place of birth.  
2. Right to Freedom: Includes freedom of speech, expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and the practice of any profession.  
3. Right against Exploitation: Prohibits human trafficking, forced labor, and child labor.  
4. Right to Freedom of Religion: Protects the freedom to practice, propagate, and manage religious affairs.  
5. Cultural and Educational Rights: Preserve the rights of minorities to conserve their culture and establish educational institutions.  
6. Right to Constitutional Remedies: Allows individuals to approach courts to protect their fundamental rights.  

These rights form the cornerstone of Indian democracy and aim to create an inclusive, just, and equitable society.  
Fundamental rights are subject to reasonable restrictions in the interest of the sovereignty, security, and public order of the country.  
They ensure that every citizen can live with dignity, freedom, and equality, forming the backbone of India’s democratic framework.
